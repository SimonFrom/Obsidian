[Katie Dill, Head of Design at Stripe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GLvFTMtw4Jk)

### Key points: 
- Avoid Zombie UI: Stop building generic, soulless interfaces. 
- Refuse to confuse done with good: Speed and polish don't equal quality. 
- Have a clear POV: Set human standards so AI doesn't default to generic outputs. 
- Encode intent: Embed design standards directly into AI workflows. 
- Embrace post-build editing: Human quality control shifts to rigorous editing at the end. 
- Raise the ceiling: Use AI to invent new interfaces, not just make old ones faster.

### 1. Have a point of view:
Without a clear point of view or direction, AI will design and create it for you. 
We as developers, not AI, need to decide what we want to convey that our app can do and feel like. 
Should it feel secure? Light? A lot of data? Very minimal?
In my opinion, a slightly wonky human made. but personal and fitting UI, is better than a "perfect" AI made one. 

### 2. Encode your standards into the machine:
Before AI we designed complete systems with UI's that worked as a whole, 'we' is the designers of course in this case. They were able to take destructured request and apply their own creative thoughts, theories and design patterns and create a brands identity as an entirely new fresh entity. 
Whereas now, with AI, which is incredibly good at looking at the past and reuse design elements, everything starts to look and feel the same. And pages are more designed in smaller bites without a brand identity to start from and refer back to.
Gutenberg wanted his books and printing to feel like it was handmade although it was machine made. That is what we should strive for, opinionated and directional design.
Instead of just designing UI components at a atomic level, design and describe user flows in detail an let agents consume that along with a style guide.

### 3. Refuse to confuse good with done:
Before the design process would weed out a lot of bad ideas, stuff that just doesn't work or just looks bad. But with the power and speed of AI, the idea is materialized almost instant. There's no marinating over decisions or multible meetings about one button. 
When UI and code is implemented, it is a lot harder to make bigger change, than to just build it "the right way" from the start. *Build* does not equal *done* and *done* does certainly not mean *good*. 


### 4. Unleash your creativity & artistry:
AI is not all bad, with it we have the option to explore a lot of different ways of doing the same thing in amazing speed.
